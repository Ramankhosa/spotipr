[0001] Background of the invention

The prior art: three weeks of drafting, per application.

[0001]The blank page problem.A disclosure arrives as a call transcript and four photos of a whiteboard. Turning it into a specification is skilled work — but most of those hours are structure, boilerplate, and formatting, not judgment.

[0002]The FER pile.First Examination Reports arrive on the examiner's schedule, not yours. Each response means re-reading the file, chasing citations, and drafting under a six-month clock.

[0003]The drawings bottleneck.Figures are drawn last, numbered by hand, and cross-referenced at midnight. One renumbered component means an evening of find-and-replace.

Claims

We claim:

1.

A method of preparing a complete patent specification, comprising: receiving an invention disclosure in any form; and returning, within hours, claims, description, and drawings ready to file.

2.

The method of claim 1, wherein the drawings generate themselves — numbered, captioned, and cross-referenced to every paragraph that cites them.

3.

The method of claim 1, wherein First Examination Reports are answered in the same workspace, with the examiner's citations in view.

4.

The method of claim 1, wherein every antecedent, reference numeral, and formatting rule is checked before export — so the objection never gets written.

5.

The method of any preceding claim, performed while the attorney does the work only an attorney can do.
