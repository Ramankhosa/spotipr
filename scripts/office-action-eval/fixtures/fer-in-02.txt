(1).   / INVENTIVE STEP:

(I)     ( )      ( )     ,     ( ) (1-9)    
  
Claim(s) (1-9) lack(s) inventive step, being obvious in view of teaching (s) of cited document(s) above under
reference for the following reasons:

Current patent application relates to a process for extracting and measuring the indole alkaloid cadambine from
the bark of the Kadamba tree. The method involves a series of steps including freeze-drying fresh bark tissues,
powdering the material, and extracting the compound using a solvent mixture of aqueous ammonia and methanol.
The invention facilitates accurate quantification via UHPLC-MS/MS using a cadambine reference standard.

D1: Kaczyski P, Lozowicka B. A novel approach for fast and simple determination pyrrolizidine alkaloids in herbs

D1 is the closest prior art.

D1 discloses an ultrasound-assisted QuEChERS-dSPE method for the extraction and quantification of
pyrrolizidine alkaloids from plant matrices, followed by LC-MS/MS analysis. D1 teaches sonication-based
extraction of alkaloids from plant material and a dispersive solid-phase clean-up using the exact combination of
C18, PSA, and GCB sorbents together with MgSO to remove pigments as well as polar and non-polar
interferences. The method further includes vortexing and centrifugation during clean-up and quantitative LC-
MS/MS analysis in MRM mode using external standards, achieving high recoveries (61-128%) with good
precision (RSD <15%). D1 anticipates the technical features of claims 1, 6, 8, and 9, and partially anticipates
claim 7 as the same sorbents are used.

D1 differs from the present application in that it is directed to pyrrolizidine alkaloids in herbs rather than
cadambine from Neolamarckia cadamba bark, employs acetonitrile instead of ammonia-methanol, omits freeze-
drying of fresh bark, does not specify the 2:2:1 sorbent ratio.

D2 describes a UHPLC-MS/MS method for indole alkaloids employing alkaline methanol (0.25% NHOH),
vibration milling, sonication, and cation-exchange SPE clean-up. D2 discloses extraction of indole alkaloids,
including from bark matrices. D2 is using methanol containing an alkaline modifier that is functionally equivalent
to aqueous ammonia, assisted by homogenization/vibration followed by sonication. The method includes solid-
phase extraction to remove matrix interferences and quantitative UHPLC-MS/MS analysis. D2 anticipates the
technical features of claim 1 and claims 9.

D2 differs from the present application in that it uses cation-exchange SPE cartridges instead of the claimed
C18/PSA/GCB combination in a 2:2:1 ratio, does not employ freeze-drying of fresh bark, lacks the specified
vortexing parameters of 2400-2600 rpm, and is applied to indole alkaloids other than cadambine from
Neolamarckia cadamba bark.

D3 provides a general review of QuEChERS methodologies applied to the extraction of functional plant
compounds, including alkaloids. D3 discloses the use of QuEChERS clean-up employing the same sorbent
combination of C18, PSA, and GCB, along with vortexing and centrifugation steps to remove pigments and both
polar and non-polar impurities. D3 anticipates the technical features of claims 1, 7, and 8, and partially claim 9.

D3 differs from the present application in that it is a general methodological review rather than a specific
analytical process for cadambine, employs acetonitrile-based extraction, lacks freeze-drying and sonication steps,
and is not directed to Neolamarckia cadamba bark.

D4 discloses an LC-MS/MS method for pyrrolizidine alkaloids using dispersive solid-phase extraction with PSA
sorbent and MgSO. D4 teaches vortexing or shaking and centrifugation during clean-up, followed by
quantitative LC-MS/MS analysis. D4 partially anticipates claims 1, 8, and 9, insofar as PSA-based dSPE clean-
up, centrifugation, and LC-MS/MS quantification are concerned.

D4 differs from the present application in that it relies solely on PSA, uses acidified methanol or acetonitrile,
omits freeze-drying and sonication, lacks the claimed sorbent ratio and vortexing parameters, and is directed to

pyrrolizidine alkaloids in feed matrices rather than cadambine from Neolamarckia cadamba bark.

D5 discloses a method for preparing cadambine from Neolamarckia cadamba using alkaline methanol and
supercritical CO extraction. D5 teaches the use of ammonia-basified methanol (pH 8-10) to extract cadambine
in its free-base form, achieving high purity and recovery (95-98.8%). D5 anticipates the technical features of
claim 1 and claim 4.

D5 differs from the present application in that it employs lengthy soaking and supercritical CO extraction rather
than vortexing and sonication, omits freeze-drying and QuEChERS clean-up, lacks the specific ammonia
concentration and operational parameters, and relates to a preparative extraction process rather than an
analytical UHPLC-MS/MS method.

Based on the collective teachings of prior art documents D1­D5, the present invention lacks an inventive step
under Section 2(1)(ja) of the Indian Patents Act. D1 establishes a complete QuEChERS-dSPE framework for
alkaloid extraction and LC-MS/MS quantification using the same C18/PSA/GCB sorbent combination with
sonication, vortexing, centrifugation, and high analytical recovery, thereby disclosing the core analytical workflow.
D2 further teaches the use of alkaline methanol and sonication for efficient extraction of indole alkaloids from
bark matrices with UHPLC-MS/MS quantification, rendering the substitution of ammonia-modified methanol and
bark substrates obvious. D3 reinforces the routine and well-established nature of QuEChERS clean-up using
the identical sorbents for plant alkaloids, while D4 confirms that PSA-based dSPE with LC-MS/MS
quantification is standard practice for alkaloid analysis. D5 specifically discloses alkaline methanolic extraction of
cadambine from Neolamarckia cadamba, thereby providing direct motivation to apply known analytical
QuEChERS-LC-MS/MS techniques to cadambine. In view of these teachings, a person skilled in the art, faced
with the problem of developing an analytical method for cadambine from Neolamarckia cadamba bark, would
have been motivated to combine the alkaline methanol extraction of D5 with the routine QuEChERS-dSPE and
LC-MS/MS methodologies of D1-D4, arriving at the claimed process without the exercise of any inventive
ingenuity. The claimed features therefore represent a mere aggregation and optimization of known techniques,
lacking any technical advancement or unexpected effect, and consequently do not involve an inventive step.

(2).   /NON PATENTABILITY:

(I)      3   (3(d) )    () (1-9)          /
Claim(s) (1-9) are statutorily non-patentable under the provision of clause ( 3(d) ) of Section 3 for the following
reasons:

Claims 1-9 of the present patent application are objected to under Section 3(d) of the Indian Patents Act, 1970,
as they merely claim the use of known processes and known reagents for extraction and quantification of
alkaloids without demonstrating any enhancement of known efficacy. The prior art documents D1­D4 already
disclose established QuEChERS/dSPE-based workflows employing sonication, vortexing, centrifugation, and
LC-MS/MS or UHPLC-MS/MS analysis for alkaloids using substantially similar sorbents (C18, PSA, GCB) and
analytical conditions, while D5 specifically discloses alkaline methanolic extraction of cadambine from
Neolamarckia cadamba. The present application merely applies these known techniques to the same compound
or closely related alkaloids, with routine modifications such as selection of solvent strength, sorbent ratios, and
operational parameters, which amount only to optimization of known processes.

The present application merely apply known alkaloid extraction and analysis processes disclosed in D1-D5 to
cadambine, without demonstrating any enhanced efficacy or technical advancement, the reported recoveries and
parameters being routine optimizations of known methods.
