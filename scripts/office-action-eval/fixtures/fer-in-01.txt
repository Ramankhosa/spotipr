Claim(s) (1-10) lack(s) inventive step, being obvious in view of teaching (s) of cited document(s) above under
reference for the following reasons:

The present invention relates to a ruthenium(II) arene­based coordination complex, namely [Ru(p-cymene)(1-
(anthracen-9-yl)-1H-naphtho[2,3-d]imidazole)Cl], and a process for preparing the same, wherein the complex
functions as a radiosensitizing antibacterial agent capable of enhancing radiation-induced cytotoxicity even at

functions as a radiosensitizing antibacterial agent capable of enhancing radiation-induced cytotoxicity even at
ultralow micro-CT-level doses and at higher therapeutic doses, including against the highly radioresistant
bacterium Deinococcus radiodurans. In contrast, D1 discloses ruthenium-based metal complexes as
radiosensitizers in general, emphasizing ROS generation and DNA damage mechanisms but without teaching
the specific Ru­anthracene­naphthimidazole structure claimed; D2 teaches ruthenium complexes engineered for
X-ray radiosensitization through ligand modification but does not disclose the claimed ligand framework; D3
discloses Ru(II) p-cymene complexes with imidazole-type N-donor ligands and routine synthetic methods similar
to the present process but lacks the anthracene-substituted naphthimidazole ligand and the claimed
radiosensitizing use; and D4 teaches metal-based radiosensitizers employing extended aromatic DNA-
intercalating ligands to enhance radiation-induced DNA damage, thereby providing design rationale but not the
specific ruthenium complex of the present invention.

Five-Step Analysis:

Person skilled in the art: A person skilled in the art for the present invention is a skilled is Medicinal chemist.

Common General Knowledge:

It is observed that the subject matter of the present invention does not go beyond the common general
knowledge of a person skilled in the art, namely a medicinal inorganic chemist, at the priority date, in view of prior
art documents D1­D4. D1 clearly establishes that ruthenium-based coordination complexes are well-known
radiosensitizers, functioning through enhancement of X-ray­induced reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation
and DNA damage, and further teaches the routine use of aromatic ligands to improve biological interaction. D2
specifically discloses ligand-engineered ruthenium complexes designed to enhance radiosensitization under X-
ray irradiation, thereby rendering the concept of modifying Ru complexes to improve radiation response part of
common general knowledge. D3 teaches Ru(II) p-cymene half-sandwich complexes coordinated with imidazole or
N-donor aromatic ligands, along with their standard synthesis from dichloro(p-cymene)ruthenium(II) dimer under
ambient conditions, which directly corresponds to the scaffold and process claimed in the present application. D4
further establishes that the use of extended -conjugated aromatic ligands, including anthracene-like systems,
for DNA intercalation and radiation-induced damage enhancement is a known and predictable design strategy in
metal-based radiosensitizers. In view of the combined teachings of D1­D4, the selection of a Ru(II) p-cymene
complex, the incorporation of an anthracene-substituted imidazole ligand for DNA binding, and the expectation of
enhanced radiosensitization represent routine design choices and workshop modifications well within common
general knowledge, rather than a technical advance involving any unexpected effect. Accordingly, the present
invention merely applies known principles and established practices in the art and does not demonstrate any
departure from common general knowledge.

Inventive concept:

The present application purports to provide a specific Ru(II) p-cymene coordination complex incorporating an
anthracene-substituted naphthimidazole ligand that combines DNA intercalation and ruthenium-mediated ROS
generation to achieve radiosensitization even at ultralow (micro-CT level) and therapeutic radiation doses,
including against highly radio-resistant organisms, whereas prior arts D1­D4 disclose ruthenium-based
radiosensitizers in general (D1, D2), Ru-p-cymene complexes with imidazole-type ligands and routine synthesis
(D3), and the use of extended aromatic DNA-binding ligands to enhance radiation-induced DNA damage (D4)
but do not disclose this specific structural combination and its claimed dual-dose radiosensitization effect.

Key difference:

The key difference of the present application over prior arts D1­D4 lies in the specific structural combination of a
Ru(II) p-cymene core with an anthracene-substituted naphthimidazole ligand, claimed to provide dual-dose
radiosensitization at both ultralow micro-CT and therapeutic radiation levels, whereas D1 and D2 disclose
ruthenium radiosensitizers in general, D3 discloses Ru-p-cymene complexes with imidazole-type ligands and
routine synthesis, and D4 teaches extended aromatic DNA-binding ligands for radiosensitization, but none
individually disclose this exact complex or its asserted dual-range radiosensitizing effect.

Obviousness:

The subject matter of the present invention is considered obvious to a person skilled in the art in view of the
combined teachings of prior art documents D1­D4 and common general knowledge at the priority date. D1
clearly establishes that ruthenium-based coordination complexes are known radiosensitizers, functioning through

teaches that modification of ligands is a routine approach to optimise radiosensitizing efficiency. D2 specifically
discloses engineered ruthenium complexes for X-ray radiosensitization, thereby providing motivation to further
tailor Ru-based systems for improved radiation response. D3 discloses Ru(II) p-cymene half-sandwich
complexes coordinated with imidazole or related N-donor aromatic ligands, prepared using standard ligand
substitution reactions from dichloro(p-cymene)ruthenium(II) dimer under ambient conditions, which directly

that would be arrived at without the exercise of any inventive ingenuity. The alleged advantages relating to
radiosensitization at ultralow and therapeutic radiation doses merely reflect the expected outcome of applying
known principles and do not arise from any unexpected technical effect attributable to a novel structural feature.
Accordingly, the present invention lacks inventive step and is obvious in view of D1 alone or at least in
combination with D2, D3, and D4.

(I)      3   (d, e )    () (1-10)          /
Claim(s) (1-10) are statutorily non-patentable under the provision of clause ( d, e ) of Section 3 for the following
reasons:

The claimed ruthenium-based complex is considered non-patentable as the application is directed to a new use
and/or property of a known class of ruthenium(II) coordination complexes. Prior art documents D1­D3 already
disclose ruthenium arene complexes with aromatic N-donor ligands and their biological activities. The present
application merely attributes an enhanced radiosensitizing and antibacterial effect, including activity at ultralow
radiation doses, to a structurally obvious variant of known ruthenium complexes, without demonstrating any
unexpected technical effect or enhanced therapeutic efficacy over the known substances. Accordingly, the claims
fall within the scope of Section 3(d) of the Patents Act, 1970 .

The subject matter of the present invention represents a mere admixture or aggregation of known components,
namely a ruthenium(II) arene core and a known aromatic DNA-intercalating ligand (anthracene-based imidazole),
each independently known in the prior art D1­D4 to exhibit radiosensitizing or biological activity. The
specification does not establish any synergistic effect arising specifically from the combination of these known
components beyond the expected additive effects of ruthenium-mediated ROS generation and ligand-based
DNA intercalation. Therefore, the claimed invention is excluded from patentability under Section 3(e) of the
Patents Act, 1970.

Therefore, the invention does not fulfill the requirements of inventive step or enhanced efficacy under Sections
3(d) and 3(e) is not patentable under the Patents Act, 1970.

(4).      /CLARITY AND CONCISENESS:

(I) () 1-10         .
Claim(s) 1-10 are not clearly worded in respect of:

The claims of the present invention are not clearly and distinctly defined as required under Section 10(5) of the
Patents Act, 1970. The expressions such as "effective radiosensitizing agent," "ultralow radiation doses," "dual-
range efficacy," and "significant biological response" are vague, subjective, and relative in nature, and no clear
technical boundaries or measurable parameters are provided to define the scope of the claims. Further, the
claims do not clearly distinguish between structural features of the ruthenium-based complex and its intended use
or functional effects, thereby rendering the scope of protection uncertain. The lack of precise definitions for
radiation dose ranges, experimental conditions, and the extent of radiosensitization makes it unclear as to what
exactly falls within or outside the claimed invention, resulting in ambiguity for both the public and enforcement
authorities. Accordingly, the claims fail to comply with the clarity and definiteness requirements of Section 10(5)
of the Patents Act, 1970.

(5).  /DEFINITIVENESS:

(I) ()1-10             () 
Claim(s) 1-10 do not sufficiently define the invention for the reasons as follows:

The claims of the present invention lack definitiveness as required under Section 10(5) of the Patents Act, 1970,
as they do not precisely define the scope of the invention. In particular, the claims rely heavily on functional and
result-oriented expressions such as "radiosensitizing agent," "antibacterial agent," and "enhances radiation-
induced cytotoxicity," without clearly limiting the invention by specific structural features or objectively verifiable
technical parameters. Additionally, essential aspects such as the exact radiation dose ranges, conditions under
which radiosensitization is achieved, and the extent of the antibacterial effect are not clearly delineated in the
claims, resulting in uncertainty as to the boundaries of the monopoly sought. The absence of clear, concrete, and
measurable claim limitations renders the claims indefinite and open-ended, thereby failing to meet the statutory
requirement of Section 10(5) of the Patents Act, 1970.
