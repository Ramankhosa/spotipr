INDEPENDENT CLAIM 1

D1 is considered to be the prior art closest to the subject matter of claim 1 and discloses the following features of
claim 1 (References from D1 are given in brackets):

A tunable triple-band terahertz metamaterial absorber device (page 2, Introduction: "a designed tunable triple-
band terahertz absorber") comprising:

a metallic ground plane to reflect incident electromagnetic waves (page 2, Model design and simulation: "a metal
substrate"; "The substrate is gold, featuring a conductivity of  = 4.6 × 10 S/m and a thickness of 0.2 m.
exceeding the skin depth of incident elec-tromagnetic waves");

a dielectric substrate layer positioned on the metallic ground plane to confine electromagnetic waves (page 2,
Model design and simulation: "dielectric layer"; " Both dielectric layer 1 and dielectric layer 2 are composed of
SiO2");

a resonator structure positioned on the dielectric substrate layer, to generate triple-band electromagnetic
absorption (Abstract: "This absorber consists of a metal substrate, a layer of graphene square rings, a layer of
graphene disks, and two layers of SiO2 dielectric sandwiched between them. Simulation results demonstrate
absorption peaks at 1.23 THz, 4.2 THz, and 6.38 THz, with respective absorption rates of 99.9 %, 99.8 %, and
98.3 %."; page 4, column 2, para 2);

and a graphene layer positioned on the resonator structure with a chemical potential adjustable through external
biasing to dynamically tune resonance frequencies in the terahertz range and to provide polarization-insensitive
absorption for incident electromagnetic waves (Abstract: " It was found that by adjusting graphene's chemical
potential, the absorption spectrum can be actively controlled. Furthermore, the absorber is insensitive to
polarization and can sustain the stability of the absorption spectrum within an incident angle range of 0~35";
Introduction: "Applying an external bias can modify the chemical potential of graphene, thereby adjusting its
surface conductivity").

However D1 do not discloses the following features:

A)The resonator structure comprises a circular base portion with a first plus-shaped extraction region and a
second plus-shaped element positioned within the first plus-shaped extraction region.

1)Regarding technical feature (A) : D1 discloses only a graphene square ring resonator and a graphene disk
resonator for generating triple-band absorption, and does not incorporate a circular base with nested plus-
shaped extraction regions and inner plus-shaped elements,

however, D2 explicitly teaches a stacked structure with crossed linear slots (plus-shaped) and four pairs of
concentric circular slots in a MoS-graphene metamaterial, achieving triple-band absorption through these
geometric features that provide polarization-insensitive performance and tunability via chemical potential
adjustment (Structures and Theoretical Model: "The complementary frequency-selective surface (FSS) structure
was used for the absorber, and its equivalent circuit model was analysed"; "The complementary frequency-
selective surface consists of crossed linear slot units and circular slot units, and the multi-frequency
characteristics are achieved by the coupling response between the different units"; "The complementary
frequency-selective surface of the absorber, consisting of two crossed linear slots and four pairs of concentric
circular slots, has three absorptions at 0.6 THz (99.7%), 1.5 THz (95.4%), and 2.5 THz (99.5%)").

Additionally, D3 discloses an inner cross pattern (plus-shaped) within an outer square ring in a VO resonator
combined with graphene, enabling switchable multi-band (including triple-band) absorption with polarization
insensitivity (Design and method: "The VO layer, with a thickness of 0.17 µm, is composed of two square rings,

an outer and an inner one. The outer ring has a gap, and a cross pattern is situated within these two rings";
Abstract: "The proposed absorber can achieve more than 99% absorption while operating in multi-band
absorption mode at 2.26, 6.68, and 7.71 THz"; "The suggested absorber also has a wide incidence angle
insensitivity").

In view of D2's clear motivation to use crossed slots and concentric circular geometries for efficient triple-band
absorption and D3's teaching that inner cross patterns within ring structures are routine for multi-band
reconfiguration, modifying D1's square ring and disk resonator to incorporate a circular base with plus-shaped
extraction regions and an inner plus-shaped element constitutes a predictable and obvious modification within the
ordinary skill of the art to optimize multi-band performance without altering the principle of operation, and
therefore does not constitute any inventiveness.

Hence, the subject matter of claim 14 lacks an inventive step because of D1, D2, D3 and common general
knowledge. Hence the same cannot be allowed u/s 2(1) (ja) of the Act.

DEPENDENT CLAIM 2-10

The subject matter of Claims 2­10 does not introduce any inventive features beyond those of independent Claim
1. These dependent claims merely define routine design variations and conventional parameter selections, such
as resonator dimensional adjustments, material choices, stacking configurations, biasing conditions, or
performance-related characteristics (e.g., bandwidth, absorption level, polarization insensitivity, or tunability),
which a person skilled in the art would ordinarily implement while designing graphene-based terahertz
metamaterial absorbers.

The claimed features represent well-known design optimisations and customary engineering refinements
commonly employed to tailor resonance frequencies, absorption strength, or tunability in metamaterial absorber
systems, and do not result in any technical advancement or unexpected technical effect beyond what is already
disclosed or rendered obvious by the prior art.

Accordingly, Claims 2­10 do not involve an inventive step and therefore cannot be allowed under Section
2(1)(ja) of the Act.
